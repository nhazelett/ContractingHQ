Beginner Track • Topic 19A

Competition Statutes for Commercial Buys

A side-by-side guide to 41 U.S.C. 1901, 41 U.S.C. 1903, 41 U.S.C. 3304, and 10 U.S.C. 3204: what each statute does, why the citation changes, and how to keep the file clean.

Statute Decoder

Same acquisition fact pattern. Different legal lane.

The confusing part is that FAR Part 12, FAR Part 6, and the U.S. Code all cross-reference each other. The clean way to teach it is to separate procedure from authority.

1 The Short Answer

Do not start with the phrase "commercial under $9M." Start with the procedure. If the acquisition is commercial and you are using FAR 12.201-1 simplified procedures, the restricted-competition authority comes from 41 U.S.C. 1901 through $9M. If the acquisition is a qualifying FAR 12.001(c) contingency, emergency, disaster, or cyber/CBRN action above $9M through $15M, cite 41 U.S.C. 1901 and 1903.

Simple rule: 1901 is the normal commercial simplified procedures authority. 1903 is the emergency expansion authority. 3304 and 3204 are the Part 6 other-than-full-and-open authorities when you are outside the Part 12 simplified procedures lane.

2 Four Statutes, Four Jobs

Part 12

41 U.S.C. 1901

Creates simplified acquisition procedures, including the commercial-product and commercial-service lane above the SAT.

Part 12

41 U.S.C. 1903

Expands thresholds for qualifying emergency, contingency, disaster, and cyber/CBRN acquisitions.

Civilian Part 6

41 U.S.C. 3304

Authorizes civilian agencies to use other than full and open competition under specific exceptions.

DoD/NASA/CG Part 6

10 U.S.C. 3204

Parallel authority for DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard to use other than full and open competition.

The statutes are not interchangeable. 1901 and 1903 explain why simplified procedures may be used. 3304 and 3204 explain why full and open competition may be restricted under Part 6.

3 Why Part 12 Still Points to Part 6

What 1901 Does

41 U.S.C. 1901 lets the FAR create special simplified procedures for commercial products and commercial services above the SAT. It also says a sole-source commercial buy under those procedures must be justified in writing and approved using the approval framework tied to 10 U.S.C. 3204(e) or 41 U.S.C. 3304(e), as applicable.

That is why the FAR overhaul tells you to prepare the written justification and get approval as described in FAR 6.104.

What That Does Not Mean

It does not mean the statutory authority for a $475K commercial simplified-procedures sole source is automatically 41 U.S.C. 3304 or 10 U.S.C. 3204.

For that action, the authority citation is still 41 U.S.C. 1901. FAR 6.104 supplies the documentation content and approval mechanics.

That cross-reference is the trap. The format may look like a Part 6 J&A, but the authority citation changes when the acquisition is in the FAR 12.201-1 simplified procedures lane.

4 What Do I Cite?

Situation Procedure Authority to Cite Why
Commercial acquisition at or below the SAT FAR 12.102(a) Document the one-source decision in the file The FAR calls for a file determination, not a full over-SAT written justification package.
Commercial acquisition above the SAT through $9M using FAR 12.201-1 FAR 12.102(b) and 12.201-1 41 U.S.C. 1901 This is the normal commercial simplified procedures lane.
Qualifying FAR 12.001(c) action above $9M through $15M FAR 12.102(b), 12.201-1, and 12.001(c) 41 U.S.C. 1901 and 1903 1903 is the special emergency procurement authority that expands the simplified procedures threshold.
Commercial acquisition above $9M that does not qualify for the $15M lane FAR 12.201-2 with Part 14 or Part 15, as appropriate One of the authorities in FAR 6.103 You are outside simplified procedures, so Part 6 authority applies if competition is restricted.
Civilian agency Part 6 only-one-source action FAR 6.103-1 41 U.S.C. 3304(a)(1) 3304 is the civilian other-than-full-and-open competition statute.
DoD, NASA, or Coast Guard Part 6 only-one-source action FAR 6.103-1 10 U.S.C. 3204(a)(1) 3204 is the defense/NASA/Coast Guard counterpart to 3304.

5 Side-by-Side Examples

Requirement Value Correct Citation File Logic
Commercial software license, only one compatible source $475K 41 U.S.C. 1901 Above SAT, below $9M, using FAR 12.201-1 simplified procedures.
Commercial logistics support for a declared contingency operation $12M 41 U.S.C. 1901 and 1903 Above $9M but within the $15M special lane because it supports a qualifying 12.001(c) circumstance.
Commercial services, sole source, not a 12.001(c) action $12M 41 U.S.C. 3304 or 10 U.S.C. 3204 Outside FAR 12.201-1 simplified procedures, so use FAR 12.201-2 and the applicable Part 6 authority.
Non-commercial specialized engineering support, only one responsible source $800K 41 U.S.C. 3304 or 10 U.S.C. 3204 Not commercial simplified procedures. Part 6 applies if restricting competition.

6 Why This Matters

The citation is not academic. It tells the reviewer which legal lane you used. If you cite 41 U.S.C. 3304 for a $475K commercial simplified-procedures action, the file reads like a Part 6 action even though Part 12 gave you a more specific commercial lane. If you cite 41 U.S.C. 1903 on every commercial action under $9M, you are invoking emergency authority when the facts do not support it.

The clean file says exactly what happened: commercial product or commercial service, simplified procedures, total value including options, whether the 12.001(c) expansion applies, and the statute that matches that lane.
