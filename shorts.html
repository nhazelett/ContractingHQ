Contracting basics

What a contract is, who can sign one, and why the government competes its work.

  • 1:00
    Episode 01

    What makes something a contract?

    Offer, acceptance and consideration. Get all three and you have a contract, even if you sold your car for a dollar.

    Watch on YouTube (Episode 1, opens in a new tab)
  • 1:39
    Episode 02

    Quote vs. proposal

    They can look like the same piece of paper, but legally they are opposites. It comes down to who is making the offer.

    Watch on YouTube (Episode 2, opens in a new tab)
  • 1:48
    Episode 03

    What counts as acceptance?

    Exact terms, the right way, from the right person. Miss one, and a contractor can deliver and never get paid.

    Watch on YouTube (Episode 3, opens in a new tab)
  • 2:06
    Episode 04

    Who can say yes for the government?

    Rank is not authority, and neither is a title. Who can bind the government to a contract, and who cannot.

    Watch on YouTube (Episode 4, opens in a new tab)
  • 1:42
    Episode 05

    What else makes a contract?

    A robber sells you a soda. Offer, acceptance and consideration make a deal, but they do not always make a contract.

    Watch on YouTube (Episode 5, opens in a new tab)
  • 2:10
    Episode 06

    CICA: why the government has to compete

    The government cannot just pick its favorite company. A 1984 law, the Competition in Contracting Act, makes it compete its contracts.
