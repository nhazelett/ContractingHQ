Standing Up

Field Ordering Officer Program

A Field Ordering Officer (FOO) program is an exception-based, tightly controlled expeditionary capability. It is not a routine substitute for normal contracting, the Government Purchase Card (GPC), or contract-ordering authority against existing vehicles. Stand one up only when the appointment is essential to the contracting mission and the lower-risk options have been ruled out. Then build it like an audit is on the way, because eventually one is.

DAFFARS 5301.603-3 AFARS 5101.602-2-92 DoD FMR Vol 5 DD 1081 / DD 2665 FAR 13.305 / 13.306
First Question

Do you actually need a FOO program?

Both DAFFARS and AFARS treat these appointments as exceptional. If a warranted contracting officer can cover the requirement, an existing Blanket Purchase Agreement (BPA) or Indefinite Delivery Indefinite Quantity (IDIQ) vehicle can support it, or a separate purchase-card ordering program already exists, those are the lower-risk paths. The FOO lane is the lane you reach for when no other lane fits.

When yes
Genuinely expeditionary need

Forward unit far from a contracting office, recurring micro-purchases for fuel, food, billeting consumables, or local-purchase items in theater. Mission tempo will not absorb the round-trip back to a warranted CO. Cash or SF 44 is the only realistic instrument. The unit can produce mission-credible candidates who are not also disbursing personnel.

DAFFARS 5301.603-3
When no
Stable, recurring requirement

If the requirement is recurring and predictable, a BPA with named callers, an IDIQ task order, or an Army Appendix EE purchase-card ordering official is the cleaner control. Migrate stable demand out of the FOO lane and into a vehicle. Leaving it in cash or SF 44 forever is how the program turns into a finding.

FAR Subpart 13.3 · AFARS App EE
When no
"We're short of warranted coverage"

Convenience is not a basis. Appointment letters issued because the office is undermanned create a control gap, not a contracting capability. Solve the staffing problem through additional warrants, augmentation, or reach-back support. Do not paper over a manning shortfall with FOO appointments.

DAFFARS 5301.603-3 (essential test)
The Build

Separation of Duties

The Paying Agent / FOO boundary

The single most important control on a FOO program is the separation between the purchasing function and the disbursing function. Combining them defeats the audit trail by design. DoD Financial Management Regulation (FMR) Volume 5 forbids it, Army legal guidance forbids it, and every audit finding in this lane traces back to it. If you take one rule away from this page, take this one.

FOO role
Purchasing officer

Identifies the source, decides what to buy, signs the SF 44 or places the BPA call, accepts the supply or service, and assembles the purchase file. This is the obligation side of the transaction.

DAFFARS 5301.603-3 · AFARS 5101.602-2-92
PA role
Paying agent

Holds advanced cash, makes the disbursement, controls custody, returns vouchers, settles accounts. This is the disbursement side. PAs are accountable officials and pecuniarily liable for funds in their possession. Appointed only when adequate payment, check-cashing, or currency-conversion services are not otherwise available.

DoD FMR Vol 5
The hard rule
One person cannot be both

DoD FMR Volume 5 states that paying agents may not act as certifying officers or purchasing officers. The FOO is the purchasing officer. So the FOO and the PA must be separate appointments held by separate people. Build the program around this constraint from Day 1, including alternate FOOs and alternate PAs to absorb leave, illness, and turnover.

DoD FMR Vol 5

Hard Limits

What a FOO cannot do

The prohibitions are concentric. Out-of-scope buys, over-the-cap buys, sub-delegation, threshold splitting, and anything that would be prohibited under a different procurement method. A FOO appointment is a license to operate inside very specific lanes, not a license to improvise.

Prohibition
Outside the subject-matter scope

DAFFARS 5301.603-3 requires the appointment letter to specify the supplies, equipment, and non-personal services covered. If the buy is outside that scope, the FOO has no authority for it. This includes drift into adjacent commodities not named in the letter.

DAFFARS 5301.603-3
Prohibition
Above the dollar cap

The appointment letter ceiling is the controlling number. Lower of the regulatory threshold, the contract limit, the theater business-clearance restriction, the funds availability, and the appointment-letter ceiling wins. Always.

FAR 2.101 (thresholds)
Prohibition
Personal services

The Air Force delegation text covers non-personal services only. A FOO appointment cannot be drafted broadly enough to authorize personal-services contracting. Personal services contracting is a separate authority with separate controls and is not in the FOO lane at all.

DAFFARS 5301.603-3
Prohibition
Sub-delegation

A FOO cannot delegate the authority to someone else. The appointment runs to the named individual and ends when that individual moves, transfers, or has the authority terminated. Need backup coverage? Appoint an alternate FOO directly. Sub-delegation by an appointee is unauthorized.

DAFFARS 5301.603-3
Prohibition
Splitting transactions

Breaking one $24,000 buy into two $12,000 buys to stay under a $15,000 threshold is splitting. It is forbidden whether the threshold is the Micro-Purchase Threshold (MPT), an appointment cap, or any other control. Auditors look for this pattern specifically.

FAR 13.003(c)
Prohibition
Bypassing other procurement rules

Air Force contingency budget guidance puts it bluntly: if a purchase is prohibited or questionable using another procurement method, it remains prohibited or questionable when the FOO uses cash, SF 44, or a card. Changing the payment method does not change the underlying compliance question.

Bottom line for the deploying CCO. Do not stand up a FOO program because the office is short on warrants. Stand one up because the mission genuinely cannot run any other way. Keep the FOO and the Paying Agent on separate letters, separate people, separate files. Set the cap, the instrument, and the scope on the appointment letter and never let any of them drift in practice. Run the stricter Air Force surveillance cadence even if you live in an Army organization. When a requirement stabilizes, migrate it out of cash or SF 44 into a BPA, IDIQ, or purchase-card ordering program. And reconcile your local theater business-clearance rules and combatant-command policy against everything above before the first letter is signed.
