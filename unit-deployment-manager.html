Unit Deployment Manager

Contracting Squadron

In a contracting squadron, Unit Deployment Manager is an additional duty. The Air Force fields a special duty career field for full-time deployment managers, with a training pipeline behind it. Additional duty UDMs get the job, the job qualification standard if somebody hands it to them, and whatever help the installation offers. This section is built for the second group.

← Back to Training

01Why this section exists

A contracting squadron UDM is usually one person carrying the duty on top of a full contracting workload. The handoff is often a binder and a phone number. Most of what gets learned comes from the Installation Deployment Readiness Cell (IDRC), the Installation Deployment Officer, and whoever held the job last.

The Air Force Job Qualification Standard for Unit Deployment Manager, dated 9 December 2025, lays out what the Air Force expects a UDM to know. It is written for the whole Air Force, so a good portion of it covers cargo movement that a contracting squadron never touches. These modules walk the parts a contracting squadron does touch, with the contracting specific pieces the standard leaves out.

The thread through all of it

The IDRC is responsible for getting members out the door. They are in the driver's seat, and the UDM is their extension inside the squadron. Being new is not a reason to stay quiet with them, and knowing a little is not a reason to stop asking. Every module here closes with what a UDM brings to that relationship and what the IDRC owns.

03Training worth chasing down

The job qualification standard lists the training an Air Force UDM works through. How much of it a contracting squadron formally holds an additional duty UDM to varies, and that is a question for the squadron and the IDRC rather than one this page can answer. Setting the paperwork aside, a few of these earn their time on their own merit.

  • The two Air Force Unit Deployment Manager distance learning modules. Both sit on myLearning. They are the closest thing an additional duty UDM gets to a schoolhouse, which makes them the best first move.
  • Two courses on the Security Training, Education, and Professionalization Portal (STEPP). One covers marking classified information, the other covers derivative classification. Both come up in real tasking work.
  • Clearance and classified network access. Worth handling at appointment rather than when a tasking lands, because a fair amount of tasking detail lives on the classified side. The Air Force personnel search tool on the classified network is one example, and the IDRC can point to what else is there.
  • Anything the squadron adds locally. Where a squadron does track the duty formally, tasks outside the standard get documented on an AF Information Management Tool (IMT) 797, the job qualification standard continuation, with a training plan behind it.
  • The miscellaneous qualifications. The standard lists increment monitor, pallet build up, weapons courier, classified courier, equipment custodian, and hazardous cargo preparation. These belong mostly to units that move cargo. Confirm with the IDRC which ones apply to the squadron, and expect the answer to be few or none.

04What these modules leave out

Three things are missing on purpose, so nobody goes looking for them.

  • Cargo and logistics machinery. Pallet build up, hazardous cargo certification, load planning, and the cargo movement systems behind them. A contracting squadron deploys people, and the units that move equipment have specialists for the rest.
  • Step by step system walkthroughs. Readiness and deployment systems are named here and explained at the level of what they do and why the data in them matters. Screens, fields, and local business rules change often enough that written walkthroughs age badly. The IDRC has the current procedures.
  • Installation specific standards. Every IDRC sets its own pre deployment standard, and the installation deployment plan is the local authority. These modules teach the process that sits underneath, so the questions asked at the IDRC are better ones.

Verify anything here against current guidance and the installation deployment plan before acting on it.
