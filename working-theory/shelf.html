Working Theory of Leadership

The shelf

The 28 books behind the pieces, and where to read them free.

All piecesThe shelfMentor’s Corner

How to get these free

Open the DoD collection in your browser, or add “Department of Defense” in Libby, choose “Sign In With My Card,” select “DoD MWR Libraries,” and authenticate with your DoD ID and birthdate. No physical library card is needed. Active duty, Guard, Reserve, retirees, and eligible sponsored family members can use the collection. DoD civilian access depends on location and resource availability. Official setup instructions and eligibility details.

Each title below links straight to the book in the DoD collection. If it isn’t there, the entry says so and links to the publisher.

New to leading

For the first months in the seat.

  • B
    Becoming a ManagerLinda A. Hill

    What actually changes when you go from doing the work to leading the people who do it, from a study of first-time managers.

    Not in DoD Libby; publisher page · Cited in Leadership for People Who Just Got Handed It, What I’ve learned over the years that you can learn right now

  • R
    Radical CandorKim Scott

    Care personally and challenge directly. The clearest book on giving feedback people can use.

    Free in Libby (audiobook) · Cited in Leadership for People Who Just Got Handed It

  • M
    MultipliersLiz Wiseman

    Why some leaders make their teams smarter and others shut them down without meaning to.

    Free in Libby (audiobook) · Cited in Leadership for People Who Just Got Handed It, What I’ve learned over the years that you can learn right now

  • T
    The First 90 DaysMichael D. Watkins

    Diagnose what you walked into before you start changing it.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in The New Rule After One Mistake

  • T
    Turn the Ship Around!L. David Marquet

    A submarine captain stops giving orders and starts giving control. Commander’s intent, told as a story.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Everything Is Priority One

Ownership, blame, and mistakes

What to do when something breaks.

  • E
    Extreme OwnershipJocko Willink and Leif Babin

    If it happened on your team, it’s yours. Combat stories with the lesson spelled out.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Blame, What I’ve learned over the years that you can learn right now

  • T
    The Dichotomy of LeadershipJocko Willink and Leif Babin

    The follow-up that adds the balance: own everything, but don’t smother your people doing it.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Leadership for People Who Just Got Handed It

  • G
    Good to GreatJim Collins

    Source of the window and the mirror: credit goes out the window, blame goes in the mirror.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Blame

  • L
    Leadership on the LineRonald A. Heifetz and Marty Linsky

    Leading gets you attacked. How to take the hit, separate your role from yourself, and stay in the game.

    Free in Libby (audiobook) · Cited in Blame

  • T
    The Right Kind of WrongAmy C. Edmondson

    Not all failures are equal. How to tell an intelligent failure from a preventable one.

    Free in Libby (audiobook) · Cited in Dropping the Hammer, What I’ve learned over the years that you can learn right now

  • T
    The Field Guide to Understanding ‘Human Error’Sidney Dekker

    Human error is a symptom, not a cause. The book behind blameless investigations.

    Not in DoD Libby; publisher page · Cited in Blame

  • W
    Whack-a-MoleDavid Marx

    The just culture model: console the honest error, coach the risky habit, punish the reckless choice.

    Not in DoD Libby; book listing · Cited in Dropping the Hammer

  • S
    Site Reliability Engineering, Chapter 15Google

    How Google writes blameless postmortems after outages. One chapter, free online.

    Free online · Cited in Blame

Culture and creativity

Why some shops feel safe to try things and others don’t.

  • T
    The Culture CodeDaniel Coyle

    How strong groups build safety, share vulnerability, and set purpose. Reads like stories.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Stop Controlling People, Change the Culture, What I’ve learned over the years that you can learn right now

  • T
    The Fearless OrganizationAmy C. Edmondson

    Psychological safety, from the researcher who named it.

    Not in DoD Libby; publisher page · Cited in Stop Controlling People, Change the Culture

  • O
    Organizational Culture and LeadershipEdgar H. Schein with Peter Schein

    The textbook on how leaders create culture through what they pay attention to. Dense, but it is the source.

    Not in DoD Libby; publisher page · Cited in Stop Controlling People, Change the Culture

  • S
    Skunk WorksBen R. Rich and Leo Janos

    How a small protected team inside Lockheed built the U-2, the SR-71, and the stealth fighter.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Why the Air Force Is Bad at Creativity

  • O
    OriginalsAdam Grant

    How people with new ideas survive inside institutions. Less risk-taking than you’d think.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Why the Air Force Is Bad at Creativity

Motivation and doing work that matters

Why people work hard, and what rewards do to that.

  • D
    DriveDaniel H. Pink

    Autonomy, mastery, and purpose beat carrots and sticks for work that takes judgment.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Make Rank Because of the Work, What I’ve learned over the years that you can learn right now

  • S
    So Good They Can’t Ignore YouCal Newport

    Stop chasing the position. Build rare and valuable skills and let the position follow.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Make Rank Because of the Work

  • T
    The Tyranny of MetricsJerry Z. Muller

    What happens when a measure becomes a target and people start working the measure.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Make Rank Because of the Work

  • G
    Give and TakeAdam Grant

    Includes the call-center study: people work harder when they meet who their work is for.

    Free in Libby (audiobook) · Cited in We’re Not Curing Cancer

Focus and doing less

Fewer priorities, fewer rules, less noise.

  • E
    EssentialismGreg McKeown

    You can only put one thing first. A case for doing fewer things better.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Everything Is Priority One

  • T
    The 4 Disciplines of ExecutionChris McChesney, Sean Covey, and Jim Huling

    Two or three goals get done. Ten don’t. A method for executing on top of the daily whirlwind.

    Free in Libby (audiobook) · Cited in Everything Is Priority One

  • S
    SubtractLeidy Klotz

    People add when they should take away. The research behind it, and how to catch yourself.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in The New Rule After One Mistake

  • T
    The Friction ProjectRobert I. Sutton and Huggy Rao

    How organizations pile up rules and steps, and how leaders clear them out.

    Free in Libby (audiobook) · Cited in The New Rule After One Mistake

Your own head

For when the problem is how hard you are on yourself.

  • T
    The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ckMark Manson

    You only have so much caring to spend. Decide what you are willing to struggle for.

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Leadership for People Who Just Got Handed It, Make Rank Because of the Work

  • C
    Can’t Hurt MeDavid Goggins

    A memoir about building tolerance for hard things. Source of “callusing your mind.”

    Free in Libby (ebook) · Cited in Blame

Research papers and articles are linked in the sources at the bottom of each piece. Libby availability checked November 2026.
