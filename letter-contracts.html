Letter Contracts

A letter contract is a written, binding instrument that lets a contractor start work before final terms are negotiated. The Government commits to a not-to-exceed (NTE) dollar amount; the parties keep negotiating the definitive contract on a clock. It exists for the moment when waiting for full pricing and full scope causes mission failure, and the file still has to track to a clean closure when the dust settles.

FAR 16.603 DFARS 217.74 NTE 180 / 40 / 50 / 75 Subpart 18.2

What it is

A letter contract is one specific type of Undefinitized Contract Action (UCA). UCA is the umbrella concept in DoD: any contract action where terms, specifications, or price are not fully agreed before performance starts. A letter contract is the written, signed, binding version, used when the Government needs the contractor to start now and there is genuinely no time to negotiate the whole agreement first.

Inside DoD, every letter contract is ordinarily managed as a UCA. That means FAR 16.603 plus the DFARS 217.74 controls layered on top.

What it is not

A letter contract is not a verbal go-ahead, an oral order, or an email telling a contractor to "start and we'll figure it out later." Those routes can produce an unauthorized commitment that has to be ratified, which is a separate problem with separate paperwork.

It is also not an unpriced purchase order. Under the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul, unpriced POs sit with the other commercial procedures in FAR Part 12. They are a small-scale tool for situations where a firm price cannot be set in advance, not the deployed answer for sustained contingency support.

Authority

Where the rules live

FAR Part 16.6 is the home address. DFARS 217.74 is the DoD overlay that adds approval, NTE, and definitization discipline. FAR Part 18.2 widens the door in declared contingencies. A letter contract does not create its own competition exception. The competition rules still come from the underlying acquisition path.

Primary Authority
FAR 16.603

Government-wide baseline. Lets the Head of the Contracting Activity (or designee) determine that no other contract is suitable. Requires the letter contract to be as complete as feasible, sets the 180-day or 40 percent definitization rule, and points at the FAR 52.216-23 / 24 / 25 clause family.

FAR Subpart 16.6
DoD Overlay
DFARS 217.74

Sits on top of FAR 16.603 for any DoD letter contract. Adds the HCA approval package, the not-to-exceed price requirement, the 50 percent obligation cap (75 percent if the contractor submits a qualifying proposal), and the qualifying-proposal consequences. DFARS 252.217-7027 replaces FAR 52.216-25 in DoD awards.

DFARS 217.74 · PGI 217.7404
Contingency Widening
FAR Part 18.2

When a contingency, emergency, or specific declaration is in place, the standing letter-contract authority opens up wider. Pointer back to Part 18 Flexibilities. Part 18 does not erase the FAR 16.603 / DFARS 217.74 controls. It changes how fast and how broadly you can reach for the tool.

Subpart 18.2 · FAR Part 16
When

When to reach for one

The first question is not "how fast can I issue a letter contract?" It is "why is a fully defined, priced action not possible in time, and what written record proves that?" If the record is weak, the action is weak. Letter contracts are emergency tools.

Use Case
Mission timing won't wait

Government interest demands an immediate binding commitment so work can start, and there is no time to negotiate a definitive contract. Storm hits a forward airfield, beddown surge, casualty response, disaster recovery. Document the operational consequence of delay in concrete terms.

FAR 16.603-2
Use Case
Requirement still forming

The customer can describe the outcome but cannot fix the full scope. Site damage assessment is incomplete, repair quantities are unknown, labor mix depends on what is found on Day 1. Pair with the strongest performance description you can write.

FAR 16.603-1
Don't Use
"We forgot to plan" buy

Letter contracts are not the cure for a customer who slept on the requirement. If the urgency basis is weak, the determination is weak, and an audit will say so. Walk back to a definitive contract or a justified bridge action instead.

FAR 16.603-3
At Award

Approvals, NTE, and the file

Three things to nail down before signature: who has to approve it, what the not-to-exceed ceiling is and how it was calculated, and what goes into the contract file the day the letter contract is awarded. The HCA approval package is where most weak letter contracts die in review.

Approvals
D&F and HCA approval

FAR requires a written determination by the HCA or designee that no other contract is suitable. DFARS adds HCA approval before entering the UCA. The package has to address the need to start before definitization, the mission impact of delay, the risks of a UCA, how those risks are mitigated, the obligation limits, and the definitization schedule.

FAR 16.603-3 · PGI 217.7404-1
Money
Not-to-Exceed (NTE) ceiling

FAR 52.216-24 caps Government liability at the dollar amount in the clause. Generally that cap covers only the contractor's funding needs before definitization, and ordinarily does not exceed 50 percent of the estimated cost without higher approval. In DoD, you must obligate no more than 50 percent of the NTE before definitization (75 percent if the contractor submits a qualifying proposal first).

FAR 52.216-24 · DFARS 217.7404-4
File
Day-one file build

At award the file needs the funded requirement package, the vehicle-selection memo, the urgency narrative, the FAR/DFARS approval package, market research, competition documentation, the Independent Government Estimate (IGE) or Rough Order of Magnitude (ROM) that supports the NTE, the signed instrument, the definitization schedule, the clause matrix, and the Contracting Officer's Representative (COR) designation. Build it before or contemporaneously with award. "Fix the file later" is how files get audited.

FAR 4.803 · FAR 1.604
After Award

Definitization, the clock is running

A letter contract that never definitizes is the failure mode every audit looks for. The schedule is set at award. The milestones drive the negotiation. The bilateral SF 30 modification that converts the letter contract into a definitive contract is the closeout step. Treat definitization like a suspense item from Day 1.

Schedule
The 180 / 40 rule, plus 50 / 75

FAR target: definitize within 180 days after the date of the letter contract or before 40 percent of the work is complete, whichever is first. DoD UCA target: the earlier of 180 days after a qualifying proposal or when obligated funds exceed 50 percent of the NTE (75 percent if the qualifying proposal is in early). Set your schedule to meet the earliest applicable milestone.

FAR 16.603-2(c) · DFARS 217.7404-3
Negotiation
Running the negotiation

Pricing follows FAR Subpart 15.4. The contractor owes a qualifying proposal with supporting data. You owe timely fact-finding, audit/pricing assistance where needed, and a documented prenegotiation objective. If the contractor submits the qualifying proposal late in DoD, you may withhold up to 5 percent of subsequent financing requests under DFARS 252.217-7027. Document why payments were or were not withheld.

FAR 15.406-3 · DFARS 252.217-7027
Closure
The definitization mod

A bilateral SF 30 modification converts the letter contract into a definitive contract. The Pre-Negotiation Memorandum (PNM) records the principal elements of the agreement. In DoD, late definitization triggers a profit reduction (the contractor has already burned off cost risk), so the weighted-guidelines record matters. Add release language for any equitable adjustments settled along the way.

FAR 43.103 · DFARS 215.404-71
The Deployed Timeline

What it looks like, hour by hour

A forward operating airfield has to absorb a humanitarian-airlift and beddown surge after storm damage in theater. The supported commander needs emergency power, billeting, dining, airfield lighting repair, latrines, debris removal, and a 60-day base operations package within 72 hours. This is what the timeline looks like for the CCO running the order under a vehicle like the Air Force Contract Augmentation Program (AFCAP).

Reference

The clause set

Pitfalls

How letter contracts go bad

A short list of how letter contracts go wrong in deployed environments. Each one has real audit history behind it. The Air Force Contract Augmentation Program (AFCAP) inspector general review found that officials did not always verify satisfactory performance before certifying payment, and missed opportunities to build stronger equitable adjustments.

Pitfall
Urgency as a paperwork substitute

Deployed teams focus on the mission and tell themselves they will fix the file later. Build the approval memo, the competition record, and the NTE rationale before or contemporaneously with award. Not after.

FAR 16.603-3
Pitfall
Unsupported NTE

Ceiling copied from a contractor estimate without independent support. NTE set arbitrarily high to "leave room," or set arbitrarily low and exhausted before definitization. Support the number with an Independent Government Estimate, historical buys, competition, or price analysis.

FAR 16.603-2(c)
Pitfall
Over-obligating before definitization

Funding actions move faster than pricing actions. Track obligations weekly against the FAR liability cap and the DoD 50 / 75 percent UCA rules. If you cross the line without a qualifying proposal, you have a finding waiting to happen.

DFARS 217.7404-4
Pitfall
Late qualifying proposal

Contractor mobilizes, then deprioritizes proposal prep. Put specific dates in the schedule. Treat proposal adequacy as a milestone with consequences. Use the 5 percent withhold under DFARS 252.217-7027 when warranted, and document the reasoning in the file.

DFARS 252.217-7027
Pitfall
Role drift

In a deployed environment, technical reps and customer leadership talk to the contractor every day. Only the contracting officer changes scope, price, or schedule. Reissue the COR delegation early. Make sure everyone knows where the line is so technical direction does not become unauthorized commitments.

FAR 1.604 · FAR 43.102
Pitfall
Invoices without acceptance evidence

Tempo is fast. Acceptance records lag. Invoices arrive. The fix: require linked receiving and acceptance support before invoice certification, every time. That is the AFCAP Inspector General (IG) finding, in one line.

FAR 32.905
Templates

Sample file language

Starter language for the four documents most commonly missing from a deployed letter-contract file. Tailor to agency supplements, HCA templates, theater business-clearance rules, and the specific vehicle. Do not paste verbatim into a real file.

Award memorandum / determination 
MEMORANDUM FOR THE CONTRACT FILE

Subject: Determination to Use a Letter Contract / UCA

I determine that use of a letter contract / undefinitized contract action
is necessary for the following reasons:

1. Immediate mission need. The requiring activity has established that
   delay in performance would materially harm mission accomplishment
   because [describe operational consequence in concrete terms].

2. Why a definitive action is not possible in time. A fully definitive
   contract action cannot be negotiated before performance must begin
   because [describe unknown quantities, site conditions, incomplete
   repair scope, missing cost drivers, or operational uncertainty].

3. Why no other contract is suitable. I have considered [definitive
   task order / bridge modification / purchase order / other vehicle]
   and determine it is not suitable because [specific reason].

4. Competition approach. The action will be [competed under fair
   opportunity / competed under FAR Part 6 / limited under urgent
   exception], and the basis for selection is [state disclosed basis].

5. Financial controls. The Government's liability / not-to-exceed
   amount is set at $[amount], which is the minimum amount necessary
   to begin performance before definitization.

6. Definitization control. The contractor shall submit its qualifying
   proposal by [date], negotiations will begin by [date], and the
   target definitization date is [date].

Signed:
[Contracting Officer]

Definitization negotiation memorandum (starter) 
Purpose of negotiation:
To definitize Task Order [number], issued on [date] as a letter
contract / UCA.

Issue summary:
At award, the Government could not accurately establish final

Key negotiation outcomes:

Bottom line for the deploying CCO. Treat a letter contract as a tightly controlled emergency tool. Preserve competition discipline. Cap liability with an NTE you can defend. Start surveillance the day of award. Drive definitization like a suspense item from Day 1. The 180 / 40 / 50 / 75 numbers are how an audit will measure your file. Memorize them.
