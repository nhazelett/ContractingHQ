Air Show in a Box · Step 5

Sole Source Builder

The director picked the act. You are the buyer. This page works out which rule covers the buy, writes the documentation from the facts you enter, and then checks it against the award record the way a reviewer would.

How to think about a single source at an air show

  • A show is a lineup of specific acts. A customer who asks for Tora Tora Tora cannot be handed an "or equal." Buying from one source is allowed when only one source is available. What the rule asks of you is the basis for that decision, in writing.
  • "The director wants them" is a preference. "No one else flies this" is a basis. So is safety: who holds the certification, who has run this airfield, who has flown with this Air Boss. Write facts a stranger could check.
  • Price is a separate finding. The single-source decision and the fair and reasonable determination are two different things. Do not use one to prove the other.
  • The money picks the rulebook. NAF and sponsorship buys follow DAFMAN 64-119, where the requesting activity writes the justification.

Safety and coordination

An air show is flown on an FAA waiver with choreographed timing. Who has worked with whom is a safety fact, and it applies to support buys as much as acts: the Air Boss runs the airspace, and the announcer and sound vendor carry crowd instructions when something goes wrong. Only one in ten justifications on record used it. Tick what is true and fill in the detail.

Market research
