FAR Part 18 Flexibilities

Every Part 18 flexibility a CCO will actually use, walked through one at a time. Two halves: the things you can always reach for, and the things that only unlock when a contingency is declared. Know which is which before you need either.

Subpart 18.1 Subpart 18.2 Letter contracts Raised thresholds Sole source

What Part 18 actually is

Part 18 is a roadmap, not a creator of new authorities. It's the consolidated list of contracting flexibilities that already exist elsewhere in the FAR, gathered in one place so a CCO under pressure isn't flipping through five different parts to remember which authority lives where.

Subpart 18.1 covers flexibilities available on any acquisition. Subpart 18.2 covers the ones that only unlock when a contingency, emergency, or specific declaration is in place.

What it doesn't do

Part 18 does not raise thresholds on its own. It does not waive a clause your contract already incorporates. It does not give you new dollar limits without an underlying declaration or determination.

Treat each flexibility below as a pointer. Go to the FAR Part it lives under, confirm the current text, and document the determination that supports your use of it.

Subpart 18.1

Always Available

Authorities a CCO can use any day of the week. No declaration required. They live in other parts of the FAR; Part 18 just collects the references so you can find them quickly when the clock is short.

Authority
Letter contracts

Authorize the contractor to start work before final terms are negotiated. The Government commits to a not-to-exceed amount; the parties keep negotiating the definitive contract. Useful when mission timing won't wait.

FAR Part 16 · Subpart 16.6
Competition
Sole source & J&A

Award without competition when only one source can meet the requirement (urgency, brand-name, only-source, follow-on continuity). Justification and Approval document required at the appropriate dollar threshold.

FAR Part 6
Schedule Use
Limited sources for FSS

Restrict fair opportunity on a Federal Supply Schedule order using one of the recognized exceptions (urgency, only-one-source, continuity, follow-on). Limited Sources Justification documents the call.

FAR Part 8
Solicitation Method
Oral solicitations

Solicit by phone, radio, or in person under simplified procedures. Document the conversation in the file: who you talked to, when, what they offered. Hard to do well on the move; harder to defend if the file is thin.

FAR Part 13
Award Path
Award without discussions

Accept proposals as-submitted and award without going into negotiation. The solicitation has to say award may be made without discussions, so this is a planning decision, not a last-minute escape hatch.

FAR Part 15
Solicitation Window
Compressed response times

Shorten the solicitation period when urgency is real and the market is known. Costs you bidders and competition strength, so be honest about the trade. Document the basis.

FAR Parts 14 & 15
Clauses
Provisions & clause tailoring

Tailor or omit certain provisions and clauses, especially in commercial procedures. Limits exist on what's tailorable; the contract still has to make sense and the file has to show why the tailoring is appropriate.

FAR Part 12
Bonds
Bid guarantees & bonds

Waive or reduce bid guarantees, performance bonds, and payment bonds where the regulation allows. Construction has its own bond rules; supply and service contracts have more room. Each waiver is risk traded for speed.

FAR Part 28
IP
Use of patented technology

Authorize use of a patented invention without prior negotiation when speed requires it. The Government may have to pay reasonable royalty later; that's the trade. Notify legal early; don't let the file go silent.

FAR Part 27
Sealed Bid Variant
Two-step sealed bidding

A sealed-bid hybrid where Step 1 evaluates technical proposals and Step 2 takes price bids only from technically acceptable offerors. Useful when full sealed bidding won't work but you want price-driven award.

FAR Part 14
Synopsis
Pre-solicitation notice

Specific exceptions to the standard public-notice requirement for proposed contract actions. Used carefully; missing a synopsis when one is required is a common protest ground.

FAR Part 5
Pricing
Cost or pricing data waivers

Waive certified cost or pricing data when commercial procedures or other exceptions apply. Reduces contractor burden and proposal time. The CO still owes a price-reasonableness determination.

FAR Part 15
Subpart 18.2

Contingency & Emergency Only

Flexibilities that unlock only when something is declared. Contingency operation, defense or recovery from certain attacks, public-health emergency, major disaster, or qualifying humanitarian or peacekeeping operation. Each declaration carries its own scope; check what you're operating under before you reach for these.

Threshold
Increased Micro-Purchase Threshold

The MPT raises significantly in declared contingencies. The exact ceiling depends on the type of operation and whether purchases are inside or outside the United States. Check current FAR Part 18 numbers before you cite a figure.

Subpart 18.2 · FAR Part 13
Threshold
Increased Simplified Acquisition Threshold

SAT also climbs in declared contingencies. More buys qualify for streamlined SAP procedures, which collapses cycle time on routine field purchases. Same rule on numbers: check current FAR Part 18 before you commit one to a memo.

Subpart 18.2 · FAR Part 13
Threshold
Increased commercial procedure ceiling

FAR Part 12 simplified procedures for commercial products and services reach into multi-million-dollar territory in declared contingencies. Bigger commercial buys without a full FAR Part 15 source selection.

Subpart 18.2 · FAR Part 12
Authority
Letter contracts in contingency

The standing letter-contract authority opens up wider in declared operations. Use it when the mission cannot wait for a definitive contract. Definitization timeline still applies; the file still has to track to closure.

Subpart 18.2 · FAR Part 16
Domestic Source
Trade Agreements & domestic preference

In specified contingencies, certain Trade Agreements Act and domestic-source restrictions can be waived or eased. Allows local-economy purchases that wouldn't be permitted in garrison procurement.

Subpart 18.2 · FAR Part 25
CBRN Response
Defense or recovery from attack

Specific flexibilities apply when the action supports defense against or recovery from chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear, or cyber attack. The declaration changes the threshold and procedure landscape sharply for those buys.

Subpart 18.2
Public Health
Public health emergency

An HHS-declared public health emergency triggers its own set of flexibilities, particularly on countermeasures, medical supplies, and health-related services. Different declaration than a contingency operation; track the source carefully.

Subpart 18.2
Disaster Response
Stafford Act activations

Major disaster or emergency declarations under the Stafford Act unlock acquisition flexibilities, including expanded set-aside provisions for businesses in the affected area. Local-area preference is a real thing here.

Subpart 18.2 · FAR Part 26
Humanitarian Ops
Humanitarian & peacekeeping

Qualifying humanitarian or peacekeeping operations carry their own subset of Part 18 flexibilities. Often paired with the increased MPT and simplified-procedure thresholds, plus eased domestic-source rules for in-theater purchases.

Subpart 18.2
Sourcing note. The exact section numbers under Part 18 keep moving as the RFO rolls out deviations. The Part itself, and the structural split between Subpart 18.1 (always available) and Subpart 18.2 (declared only), is stable. Go to FAR Part 18 on acquisition.gov for current numbers. If a flexibility looks different from how this page describes it, the FAR wins.
